# Equity for Humanity — Global Human-Flourishing Index Proposal v0.1

> **Working proposal for discussion**
> **Status:** Equity for Humanity is a public concept, not a registered fund, investment product, securities offering, or source of personal investment advice. This proposal does not direct any real-money investment, accept capital, promise returns, or replace legal, fiduciary, tax, licensing, or regulatory advice.

## The simple recommendation

**Use the FTSE Global All Cap Index as Equity for Humanity’s v0.1 *reference universe*.**

That means: start with a broad, investable public-market representation of large, mid, and small companies across developed and emerging markets; use market-capitalization weights; then add a **small, transparent human-flourishing safeguard and stewardship layer**.

The name is **FTSE**, not “FTSC.”

The closest equally credible alternative is **MSCI ACWI IMI**. Both are broad global all-cap universes. FTSE Global All Cap is proposed here because it already appears in Equity for Humanity’s founding concept and has clear published rules for global coverage, investability, review, and eligible new listings. MSCI ACWI IMI should remain the principal cross-check/fallback — not a rejected alternative.

> **The core idea is not to guess tomorrow’s winners. It is to own a broad share of productive public enterprise, so the fund has a chance to participate when tomorrow’s winners emerge.**

No index can guarantee that outcome. Broad indexing will still own companies that struggle, and a values screen can still exclude a future winner. The goal is a more honest tradeoff: **maximum broad ownership first; carefully bounded mission safeguards second.**

---

## 1. Why this fits Equity for Humanity

Equity for Humanity’s founding concept already says the goal is not to pick AI winners, but to own productive global growth broadly, efficiently, and transparently as technology permeates the world economy.

This proposal keeps that promise.

### What we are trying to achieve

- **Global:** developed and emerging public markets, rather than one country or one technology theme.
- **All-cap:** small, medium, and large public companies, not only today’s mega-caps.
- **Self-updating:** companies that grow can become bigger positions; companies that shrink become smaller positions; new eligible public companies can enter under published rules.
- **Simple:** one understandable core universe rather than a secret stock-picking system.
- **Human-centred:** avoid clearly defined severe harm and use ownership to press for better conduct.
- **Trustworthy:** every rule, source, exception, vote, and change should be visible and challengeable.

### The critical insight about future winners

Long-run stock-market wealth creation has historically been concentrated in a small minority of companies. That is one reason a narrow portfolio can miss the businesses that create the most value.

But it does **not** follow that we can reliably screen for the winners now. A company may look unprofitable before it becomes important; a company with excellent current metrics may later fade. For the core, broad exposure is more honest than pretending that a committee, an AI, or an ESG score can foresee the future.

## 2. The proposed framework: broad ownership + humanity safeguards

### Layer A — The Global Productive Economy Core

**Reference universe:** FTSE Global All Cap Index.

**Core rule:** hold each eligible constituent in proportion to its free-float-adjusted market capitalization, as supplied by the licensed/provider methodology or a legally approved replicating method.

**Why this rule is simple and powerful**

- The core holds large, mid, and small public companies.
- It includes developed and emerging-market exposure.
- It does not force the fund to make individual growth forecasts.
- It naturally reflects market evolution: successful businesses generally become larger positions; fallen businesses generally become smaller positions or leave under the provider’s rules.
- It has a defined route for eligible IPOs/new public companies to enter.

**Important limitation:** “global” does not mean every business on Earth. It means eligible, investable listed securities in the provider’s covered markets. Private companies, many frontier-market issuers, very small or illiquid securities, and restricted holdings may not be included.

### Layer B — The Human-Flourishing Safeguard and Stewardship Layer

The safeguard layer should be **narrower than the core**. It should prevent clearly incompatible ownership while avoiding a sprawling, opaque system that silently turns a global index into a concentrated thematic bet.

#### v0.1 operating principle

> **Exclude only what Equity for Humanity can define, evidence, publish, review, and defend. Engage where ownership can credibly improve conduct. Do not call a company “good” merely because a vendor gave it a high score.**

### Proposed policy architecture

| Element | v0.1 proposal | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Starting universe | FTSE Global All Cap reference universe. MSCI ACWI IMI is a published comparator/fallback. | Keeps the core global, all-cap, and understandable. |
| Weighting | Free-float-adjusted market-cap weights; no discretionary security selection in the core. | Retains broad exposure and lets markets, not a committee, set relative scale. |
| Size / geography | Preserve large, mid, and small-cap representation across developed and emerging markets as provided by the underlying universe. | Avoids a hidden mega-cap, U.S.-only, or theme-only fund. |
| New public companies | Follow the reference universe’s published addition/review/fast-entry rules. Do not hand-pick IPOs. | Makes room for future public winners without hype-driven discretionary buying. |
| Hard safeguard | A short, published set of severe incompatibilities: investments prohibited by applicable law; clearly defined controversial-weapons involvement; and severe, well-evidenced, persistent failures of fundamental human-rights, labour, environmental, or anti-corruption responsibilities where remediation is absent. | Creates a moral floor without claiming perfect knowledge or making a broad political blacklist. |
| Evidence standard | Use named sources, dated evidence, documented materiality thresholds, conflict checks, and an independent review record. No single commercial ESG rating can decide a case. | Makes exclusions contestable and reduces greenwashing/data-vendor dependence. |
| Remediation | For conduct cases, give notice, publish the concern where lawful, seek credible remediation, and review on a published timetable before/after exclusion except when law or extreme harm requires immediate action. | Preserves fairness, due process, and the chance to improve behaviour. |
| Stewardship | Where retained, vote and engage on material human-rights, labour, environmental, governance, disclosure, and anti-corruption issues. Publish a stewardship and voting record where legally possible. | Ownership can be a tool for improvement, not only a passive financial claim. |
| Optional impact tilt | **Not part of the core in v0.1.** A later small, capped positive-solutions sleeve may be considered only after a paper-index test, independent review, cost/tracking-error analysis, and explicit governance approval. | Prevents the mission from sacrificing broad ownership for an untested story. |

## 3. What belongs in the safeguard layer — and what remains a decision

### Proposed minimum responsibility floor

The UN Global Compact’s Ten Principles are a useful common language for the four categories below:

1. human rights;
2. labour;
3. environment; and
4. anti-corruption.

They are a **floor**, not a certificate of goodness.

A future written rulebook should distinguish among:

- **illegal/prohibited investment** under the fund’s governing jurisdiction;
- **direct, material involvement in activities the governance body has explicitly found irreconcilable with human flourishing**;
- **severe and persistent conduct failure** supported by credible evidence and not credibly remediated; and
- **ordinary controversy, imperfect disclosure, or remediable operational weakness**, where engagement and transparency may be better than exclusion.

### Choices that should not be hidden in a score

Equity for Humanity must decide — publicly — where it stands on categories such as fossil fuels/thermal coal, tobacco/nicotine, alcohol, gambling, civilian firearms, private detention, defence, surveillance, and high-interest consumer lending.

This proposal deliberately does **not** choose all of those lines for David or for future global governance. They involve real tradeoffs, different legal contexts, and consequences for diversification. What matters at this stage is the rule:

> **No category enters the exclusion list until the exact definition, materiality threshold, evidence source, remediation treatment, review cycle, and rationale are written down and publicly approved.**

That is stricter and more trustworthy than a marketing label such as “sustainable” or “ethical.”

## 4. Why not simply buy only growing, profitable, high-scoring companies?

Because that would undermine the goal of owning the future broadly.

A hard financial-quality filter can be useful for research, risk monitoring, or a later satellite experiment. It should not automatically control the core because it may:

- sell or avoid companies before their breakthrough years;
- favor mature incumbents over younger innovators;
- create a large hidden tilt toward particular countries, sectors, or accounting styles;
- raise turnover and cost; and
- turn a universal-ownership foundation into a manager’s forecast portfolio.

The v0.1 answer is therefore deliberately modest:

- **Core:** global all-cap, market-weighted ownership.
- **Safeguard:** narrow, evidence-led exclusions plus stewardship.
- **Scorecard:** public monitoring of financial durability, useful-product/service hypotheses, conduct, labour, environmental footprint, governance, and controversy — initially **not** a weighting algorithm.
- **Optional later research:** test a small quality/positive-solutions tilt against the core, transparently and before real capital is affected.

## 5. How the framework stays current

### Market-maintenance rules

The reference index provider handles ordinary market maintenance: eligibility, free float, corporate actions, country classifications, reviews, and qualifying new issues. FTSE’s published rules use regular reviews and buffers intended to limit unnecessary turnover.

Equity for Humanity should not try to duplicate that machinery at concept stage. A future implementation would need licensed data and a regulated/qualified implementation partner.

### Safeguard-maintenance rules

A future E4H rulebook should set this cadence:

| Activity | Proposed minimum cadence | Public output |
|---|---:|---|
| Reference-universe updates | Follow underlying provider’s published schedule | Provider reference/version and implementation date |
| Safeguard data refresh | At least quarterly, plus prompt review of credible severe events | Timestamped change log |
| Conduct-case review | Prompt triage; scheduled full review at least annually | Case status, evidence standard, decision and rationale where lawful |
| Rulebook review | Annual, with public consultation for material changes | Version history and redline/summary |
| Stewardship/voting report | At least annually; material votes/events disclosed sooner where feasible | Voting and engagement report |
| Independent assurance | Annual once assets/operations justify it | Scope, findings, conflicts, and limitations |

### Transparency requirements

At a mature stage, publish where legally and contractually permitted:

- the reference benchmark and version;
- all holdings, weights, and portfolio-level country/sector/size exposures;
- every exclusion and inclusion decision, with applicable rule and date;
- data sources, confidence/limitations, and conflicts;
- benchmark-relative tracking difference, costs, turnover, and tax/implementation effects;
- voting and engagement records; and
- complaints, appeals, corrections, and rule changes.

## 6. Anti-greenwashing safeguards

A good name is not proof of a good system. The following safeguards are non-negotiable in any future implementation.

1. **Public rulebook, not vibes.** Publish definitions, thresholds, sources, decision rights, and review cadence.
2. **Version history.** Every rule change must have a dated rationale and effective date.
3. **Independent, conflict-aware oversight.** A founder, donor, vendor, or fund manager should not be the sole judge of a company affecting their interests.
4. **No single-score morality.** ESG and impact datasets may inform research, but divergent methodologies and incomplete disclosure must be visible.
5. **Evidence and appeal.** Companies and affected stakeholders need a clear way to correct factual errors or present remediation evidence; appeals must not be a pay-to-play route.
6. **Engagement before exclusion where appropriate.** Exclusion is not the only responsible action. Retain and use ownership when it offers a realistic path to improvement.
7. **No unsupported impact claims.** Say “screened under this rule” or “engaged on this issue,” not “made the company good” or “guaranteed social impact.”
8. **Report the cost of values.** Publish tracking difference, turnover, concentration, exposure shifts, and implementation cost relative to the broad reference universe.
9. **Independent review.** At an appropriate scale, commission periodic assurance of methodology, data controls, conflicts, and reporting.

## 7. The tradeoffs we accept openly

| Choice | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Broad all-cap core | Better chance of owning rare future winners; broad diversification; simple explanation. | Also owns companies that later fail or disappoint. |
| Market-cap weighting | Low turnover, scalable, self-updating relative sizes. | Gives today’s largest companies the largest weights; may look concentrated at times. |
| Narrow conduct safeguards | Clearer moral floor; easier audit; lower risk of hidden style bets. | Will not satisfy every ethical preference; requires difficult public boundary decisions. |
| Engagement/stewardship | Keeps a voice inside companies; can support measurable improvement. | Slow, imperfect, resource-intensive, and not guaranteed to work. |
| No v0.1 positive-impact tilt | Protects simplicity and broad ownership. | Does not deliberately overweight solutions businesses yet. |
| Transparent data rules | Builds trust and enables challenge/correction. | Costs more and may reveal uncertainty instead of offering comforting certainty. |

## 8. Safe staged path — research before money

### Stage 0 — Founding research (now)

- Publish this discussion proposal and research notes.
- Invite critique from investors, public-benefit leaders, governance experts, human-rights/labour/environment specialists, and people from different regions.
- Do not take money or call this an investment product.

### Stage 1 — Internal paper index and scorecard

- Create a *paper-only* benchmark-relative scorecard using licensed/permitted data.
- Test alternative narrow safeguard definitions against FTSE Global All Cap and MSCI ACWI IMI.
- Measure holdings coverage, sector/country shifts, turnover, estimated cost, tracking difference, controversy outcomes, and the practical quality of data/appeals.
- Publish what the test can and cannot show. Historical results are not a forecast.

### Stage 2 — Governance and legal design

Before any public activity, obtain qualified advice on:

- charitable/nonprofit/trust/public-benefit structure;
- securities, investment-management, adviser, and marketing rules in relevant jurisdictions;
- custody, audit, fiduciary duty, conflicts, tax, sanctions, AML, privacy, and reporting;
- benchmark/data licensing and trademark use; and
- whether a regulated partner should implement the strategy instead of Equity for Humanity doing so directly.

### Stage 3 — Independent review and controlled pilot

Only after governance, legal structure, custody, disclosures, and professional responsibility are in place should an independent body decide whether any small, controlled, lawful pilot is justified. Existing low-cost global index solutions may remain the safer implementation route; creating a proprietary fund/index is **not** the default.

## 9. Decisions for David and future governance

These are the questions to resolve through back-and-forth — not silently through an algorithm.

1. **Reference universe:** Confirm FTSE Global All Cap as the v0.1 reference universe, with MSCI ACWI IMI as the formal comparator/fallback?
2. **Moral floor:** Which activities are clearly irreconcilable with human flourishing in every future implementation jurisdiction, and which should be left to engagement or local legal context?
3. **Materiality:** What revenue/ownership/operational threshold should make an activity material enough to trigger a review or exclusion?
4. **Remediation:** What evidence and time window should allow a company to remain, return, or be removed after a serious conduct finding?
5. **Engagement:** When should the fund retain ownership to vote and engage, and when does continuing ownership become inconsistent with the mission?
6. **Positive impact:** Should E4H ever create a capped, separately reported solutions tilt — or should it keep the core completely market-weighted forever?
7. **Governance:** Who has decision rights, how are conflicts managed, and how can participants or affected communities challenge decisions?
8. **Implementation:** Is the right long-term path a regulated partner, a separately licensed index, existing low-cost funds for the endowment, or no dedicated investment vehicle at all?
9. **Transparency vs privacy:** What is the right level of holdings, engagement, and case disclosure without creating legal, security, or privacy harms?

---

## 10. Bottom line

Equity for Humanity does not need to pretend it can know the future. The strongest simple starting point is to **own it broadly**.

Use FTSE Global All Cap as the global all-cap reference universe. Keep the core market-weighted and low-complexity. Add a limited, public, evidence-led safeguard layer for severe incompatibilities. Use stewardship where ownership can improve conduct. Test any more ambitious impact or quality tilt on paper first.

> **Broad ownership gives humanity a share in the productive future. Transparent safeguards help ensure that the way we own it remains worthy of humanity.**
