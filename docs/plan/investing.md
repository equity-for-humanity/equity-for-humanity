# Investing

> **Not financial advice.** Equity for Humanity holds no funds. The investment approach is set by the [Investment Proposal](../planning/e4h-global-human-flourishing-index-proposal-v0.2.md) and the [Founding Concept Brief](../current/equity-for-humanity-founding-concept-v1.0.md). This page addresses practical implementation questions for a future governing body. Figures were checked on 2026-09-24 and change frequently.

## In brief

- **The approach is set by the Investment Proposal.** The reference universe is the FTSE Global All Cap Index, with market-capitalization weights. Companies that Sustainalytics Global Standards Screening classifies as non-compliant with the UN Global Compact's Ten Principles are omitted. There is no stock picking, sector tilt, or activist programme.
- **No off-the-shelf fund applies this screen.** Broad index funds track the unscreened index, and ESG-labelled funds apply wider exclusions and often narrower coverage.
- **Implementation is a decision for a future governing body.** Before any capital is invested, the project will carry out the paper implementation described in the proposal, obtain advice, and leave the final decision to a properly constituted board.
- **Safeguards come first.** The fund is to be held by the legal entity at a regulated institution, with dual approval for every transaction and full public disclosure of holdings.

## The investment approach

The Investment Proposal sets out the policy. In summary:

| Element | Position in the Investment Proposal |
|---|---|
| Reference universe | FTSE Global All Cap Index: developed and emerging markets; large, mid, and small companies |
| Weighting | Free-float market-capitalization weights; no discretionary selection |
| Conduct screen | Omit companies that Sustainalytics Global Standards Screening classifies as non-compliant with the UN Global Compact's Ten Principles, regardless of size |
| Reinstatement | A company returns at the next quarterly review once it is no longer classified as non-compliant |
| Other exclusions | None; no industry, lifestyle, or political screens |
| Ownership | Light-touch; any voting follows a disclosed default policy, subject to law and fiduciary duties |
| Transparency | Full holdings, every exclusion and reinstatement, costs, and data-coverage limits are published |

The UN Global Compact's Ten Principles cover human rights, labour, the environment, and anti-corruption ([UN Global Compact, the Ten Principles](https://unglobalcompact.org/what-is-gc/mission/principles)).

- **Canadian all-in-one funds hold far more Canadian companies than Canada's share of world markets.** The Investment Proposal calls for market weights, which a fund with a deliberate home-country weighting does not provide.

## Implementation options for a future board

| Option | Description | Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Licensed index with the screen applied by a regulated manager** | Obtain authorized FTSE Global All Cap data and Sustainalytics status data, and have a registered portfolio manager implement the screened universe. | Closest to the proposal. Data licensing and management costs are **[UNVERIFIED]** and may be significant for a small fund. |
| **Direct holding of a large sample** | Hold a representative sample of the screened universe directly. | Trading, custody, and corporate actions are complex. The proposal notes that agents do not eliminate these costs. |
| **Broad fund with the screen reported on paper** | Hold a broad fund temporarily and publish which companies the screen would omit. | Does not implement the screen. It could be used only if the board decides so, with full disclosure, because the proposal states that an unscreened product does not match its rule. |
| **Cash until implementation is approved** | Hold contributions in cash or cash equivalents until the board approves an implementation. | Simplest and safest in the early stage. Delays compounding. |

The Investment Proposal lists these questions as matters for a future governing body, once the legal structure exists.

## Tax considerations

- **Withholding tax on foreign dividends.** Many countries, including the United States, withhold tax on dividends paid to foreign investors. Treaty exemptions for Canadian registered charities exist ([IRS, Canadian charities under the treaty](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/eotopicc01.pdf)). Because the core fund is planned as a non-charity structure, these exemptions are unlikely to apply **[UNCERTAIN]**.
- **Holding through Canadian pooled funds.** In August 2026, a US court ruled that a Canadian charity holding US shares through a Canadian pooled fund open to non-exempt investors did not qualify for the treaty exemption ([Torys LLP summary](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/09/implications-for-canadian-exempt-organizations-earning-us-source-income)). This mainly concerns charities, but shows that the choice of investment vehicle can have tax consequences.
- **Tax status of the entity.** Whether a not-for-profit whose main activity is investment remains tax-exempt is uncertain. See [legal structure](legal-structure-options.md).

These points require advice from an accountant before any investment is made.

## Questrade Custom Indexing

In June 2026, Questrade launched Custom Indexing. It allows an investor to build an index of up to 600 securities, held directly as fractional shares, with no commissions or platform fee. When holdings drift more than 10% from their targets, the investor receives an alert and can confirm a rebalance ([Questrade, how Custom Indexing works](https://www.questrade.com/learning/using-questrade/how-custom-indexing-works); [what Custom Indexing is](https://www.questrade.com/learning/investing-basics/what-is-custom-indexing); [costs and risks](https://www.questrade.com/learning/accounts-taxes/benefits-risk-personalized-index-portfolios); [BrokerGuide](https://www.brokerguide.ca/blog/questrade-custom-indexing)).

| Capabilities | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Build a custom list and apply exclusions | A maximum of 600 securities, compared with about 8,000 to 10,000 in a global all-cap index |
| Own shares directly, with voting rights | US-listed securities only at present; currency conversion costs apply |
| Rebalance without commissions | Each rebalance must be confirmed by a person |
| Low minimum investment | Offered for personal registered and cash accounts; availability to organizations is **[UNVERIFIED]** |
| | Not designed to manage money on behalf of others or to pool contributions |

**Assessment:** useful for studying how a screen changes a portfolio. It does not suit the Humanity Fund, because it is limited to US securities and 600 holdings and does not appear to be available to organizations.

**Accounts for organizations:** Questrade offers corporate accounts that directors or officers can open with the business number, articles of incorporation, a corporate resolution, and identification for each director and signing officer ([Questrade corporate accounts](https://www.questrade.com/learning/investing-basics/corporate-accounts)). Other Canadian brokerages offer comparable accounts once an entity exists.

## Custody and management

- **Custody:** the fund is held in the legal entity's own account at a regulated Canadian institution, never in a personal account.
- **Registration:** an organization investing its own assets generally does not need securities registration. Managing others' money as a business, or operating a pooled investment fund, can require registration under National Instrument 31-103 ([ASC guidance](https://www.asc.ca/en/investment-funds/how-do-the-registration-requirements-apply-to-investment-funds); [31-103 companion policy](https://www.asc.ca/-/media/ASC-Documents-part-1/Regulatory-Instruments/2026/01/6258977v1-31-103-CP-Consolidation-Eff-January-1-2026.pdf)).
- **Design implication:** contributors give their contributions to the fund and receive no financial return. Participants receive distributions but do not hold units or individual balances in the fund. Displaying a personal "share of the fund" balance could create the impression of an investment product **[UNCERTAIN; to be reviewed by a securities lawyer]**. The prototype's wording should be reviewed with this in mind.

## Contributions other than cash

The Founding Concept Brief anticipates accepting cash, public securities, crypto-assets, and other approved contributions, which are then stewarded or divested under transparent rules. Practical options in Canada are currently limited: CanadaHelps, one of the few Canadian platforms that processed crypto donations, has paused that service ([CanadaHelps](https://www.canadahelps.org/en/cryptocurrency/15251/donate)). Common practice is to convert crypto-assets promptly and record them at fair market value ([Charity Law Group](https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/can-charities-in-canada-accept-cryptocurrency-donations)). Rules for each type of contribution will be published before it is accepted.

## Draft investment and custody policy

This draft is prepared for a future board, based on the founding documents and common practice ([Carters, drafting investment policies](https://carters.ca/pub/seminar/charity/2025/CBA_Charity_Law_Conference-Drafting_General_Investment_Policies.pdf); [GiveWise investment policy statement](https://guide.givewise.ca/policy-and-user-documents/investment-policy-statement.md)).

1. **Purpose.** The Humanity Fund (Bucket A) is invested for long-term growth, and a portion of the returns is distributed under the payout rule in the Founding Concept Brief. The Stewardship and Operations Reserve (Bucket B) is held and reported separately.
2. **Before implementation.** Until the board approves an implementation of the Investment Proposal, contributions are held in cash or cash equivalents at a regulated Canadian institution.
3. **After implementation.** The fund follows the Investment Proposal: the FTSE Global All Cap reference universe, market weights, and the UN Global Compact conduct screen.
4. **Prohibited.** Leverage, derivatives used for speculation, discretionary stock selection, and any investment connected to a director, contributor, or staff member.
5. **Contributed assets.** Securities, crypto-assets, and other approved assets are stewarded or divested under published rules.
6. **Liquidity.** Enough cash is held to meet expected distributions and operating commitments for a period set by the board.
7. **Authority.** Every trade or transfer requires the approval of two directors. No AI agent may place trades or move funds.
8. **Reporting.** Holdings, exclusions, costs, and transactions are published at least quarterly.
9. **Review.** The policy is reviewed annually, and immediately after any change to the legal structure.
