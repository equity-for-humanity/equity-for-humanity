# Investing

> **Not financial advice.** E4H holds no money today. This page explains the options so a future board can adopt a simple, safe investment policy. Figures were checked on 2026-09-24 and change often.

## The short answer

- **Early stage:** hold new money in cash until it passes a small threshold, then buy **one broad global stock ETF**. Nothing else.
- **Which ETF** depends on the legal structure and on tax. A Canadian charity may do better holding a US-listed global fund directly than a Canadian-listed one (see "The tax surprise" below). Ask an accountant before the first purchase.
- **The E4H screen** (excluding companies that break UN Global Compact principles, from the [index proposal](../planning/e4h-global-human-flourishing-index-proposal-v0.2.md)) is not available in any off-the-shelf fund. Keep it as a later goal, reported "on paper" until the fund is big enough to implement it properly.

**"Home bias":** the Canadian all-in-one funds hold 24 to 31% Canadian companies, even though Canada is only a few percent of the world market. For a fund meant to belong to all of humanity, a market-weighted fund like VT is a better match to the mission.

### Screened (ESG) funds

- **GEQT** (iShares ESG Equity ETF Portfolio): MER 0.25%. Excludes tobacco, controversial weapons, companies in severe controversies, and the lowest-rated ESG companies in each sector. It has **no emerging-markets part**, so it leaves out much of the world. ([iShares GEQT](https://www.blackrock.com/ca/investors/en/products/315678/ishares-esg-equity-etf-portfolio-fund); [Greenline explainer](https://usegreenline.com/articles/geqt-etf-explained))
- **None of these match E4H's rule.** E4H wants to exclude only companies that a data provider rates as non-compliant with the UN Global Compact, and to keep everything else, including emerging markets. Off-the-shelf ESG funds exclude much more and cover less.
- **Recommendation:** start with a plain global fund. Publish a quarterly "paper" report of which companies the E4H screen *would* remove. Implement the real screen later through a regulated manager, when the fund is large enough to justify data licensing costs **[UNVERIFIED cost of FTSE and Sustainalytics data licences; likely not public]**.

## The tax surprise: where the fund is held matters

US companies pay dividends, and the US normally withholds tax on dividends paid to foreigners. A Canadian **registered charity** can be exempt from that tax under the Canada-US tax treaty when it holds US investments **directly** ([IRS, Canadian charities under the treaty](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/eotopicc01.pdf)).

On 25 August 2026, a US court ruled in *South Saskatchewan Community Foundation v. United States* that a Canadian charity holding US stocks **through a Canadian pooled fund** (a unit trust open to non-exempt investors) did **not** get that exemption ([Torys LLP summary](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/09/implications-for-canadian-exempt-organizations-earning-us-source-income); [Tax Interpretations summary](https://taxinterpretations.com/content/1137222)).

Canadian-listed ETFs like XEQT and VEQT are generally structured as Canadian trusts, so the ruling may apply to them. A US-listed fund like VT, held directly, may qualify for the exemption with the right form (W-8EXP) **[UNCERTAIN; the ruling may be appealed and details matter]**. On a fund yielding about 1.5%, 15% withholding costs about 0.2% a year, which is more than the fund's own fee. **Get an accountant's view before choosing.**

A non-profit that is not a registered charity would likely not get this exemption at all **[UNCERTAIN]**.

## Questrade's Custom Indexing: what it can and cannot do

Questrade launched **Custom Indexing** in June 2026 ([BrokerGuide](https://www.brokerguide.ca/blog/questrade-custom-indexing)). You build your own index of up to 600 stocks, owned directly as fractional shares, with no commissions and no platform fee. When holdings drift more than 10% from targets you get an alert and rebalance with one click. It can even be set up by an AI assistant (Claude) through Questrade's connector, for the user's review and approval. ([How it works](https://www.questrade.com/learning/using-questrade/how-custom-indexing-works); [What it is](https://www.questrade.com/learning/investing-basics/what-is-custom-indexing); [Costs and risks](https://www.questrade.com/learning/accounts-taxes/benefits-risk-personalized-index-portfolios))

| It can | It cannot (today) |
|---|---|
| Build a custom list and apply E4H-style exclusions yourself | Hold more than 600 securities (a global all-cap index has 8,000 to 10,000) |
| Own shares directly, with voting rights | Hold non-US stocks: the current universe is US-listed securities only, so currency conversion fees apply |
| Rebalance in one click with no commissions | Rebalance automatically: a person must confirm each rebalance |
| Start from $10 | It is offered in TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, and personal cash accounts. **Organization (corporate) accounts are not listed [UNVERIFIED]** |
| | Manage money for other people, pool donors' money, or issue tax receipts |

**Verdict:** a useful personal learning tool and a way to prototype the screen, but **not suitable for the Humanity Fund** at this stage. It is US-only, capped at 600 stocks, and apparently not available to organizations.

**Organization brokerage accounts:** Questrade does offer corporate accounts. Only directors or officers can apply, and they need the business number, articles of incorporation, a corporate resolution, and ID for every director and signing officer ([Questrade corporate accounts](https://www.questrade.com/learning/investing-basics/corporate-accounts)). Qtrade's forms also cover charities and unincorporated groups ([Qtrade forms](https://www.qtrade.ca/en/investor/apply/forms.html)). Any major Canadian discount brokerage should work once E4H has a legal entity.

## Custody and who may manage the money

- **Custody** means who legally holds the investments. For E4H: the legal entity's own brokerage account, at a regulated Canadian dealer. Never a personal account.
- **Managing your own money** generally does not need securities registration. Managing other people's money as a business, or running a pooled investment fund, can require registration as a portfolio manager or investment fund manager under National Instrument 31-103 ([ASC guidance](https://www.asc.ca/en/investment-funds/how-do-the-registration-requirements-apply-to-investment-funds); [31-103 companion policy](https://www.asc.ca/-/media/ASC-Documents-part-1/Regulatory-Instruments/2026/01/6258977v1-31-103-CP-Consolidation-Eff-January-1-2026.pdf)).
- **What keeps E4H safe:** the fund belongs to the organization. Donors give it away and get nothing back. Participants receive payouts but do not own units or balances in the fund. **Showing people a personal "share of the fund" balance** could make it look like an investment fund **[UNCERTAIN; ask a securities lawyer]**. The prototype app's wording should be reviewed with this in mind.
- **Later,** when the fund is large, the board can hire a registered portfolio manager to run the screened index for a fee.

## Crypto donations

Canadian charities can accept crypto as a gift-in-kind at fair market value, but most experts recommend converting it to Canadian dollars right away ([Charity Law Group](https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/can-charities-in-canada-accept-cryptocurrency-donations)). CanadaHelps, one of the few Canadian platforms that processed crypto gifts, has **paused** that service ([CanadaHelps](https://www.canadahelps.org/en/cryptocurrency/15251/donate)). E4H should not accept crypto until a reliable, compliant processor is available, and should never hold it.

## Draft early-stage investment policy

This is a starting draft for the future board, based on common charity practice ([Carters, drafting investment policies, 2025](https://carters.ca/pub/seminar/charity/2025/CBA_Charity_Law_Conference-Drafting_General_Investment_Policies.pdf); [GiveWise investment policy example](https://guide.givewise.ca/policy-and-user-documents/investment-policy-statement.md)).

1. **Purpose.** The Humanity Fund (Bucket A) is invested for long-term growth to fund payouts under the approved payout rule. The stewardship reserve (Bucket B) is kept separate.
2. **What we may hold.** Cash in a Canadian bank account or a high-interest savings account, and **one** broad global stock index ETF chosen by the board. Nothing else.
3. **What we may not hold.** Individual stocks, leverage (borrowing), derivatives, crypto, private companies, or anything connected to a director, donor, or staff member.
4. **When to invest.** Keep new money in cash until the uninvested amount reaches a threshold set by the board (for example $5,000), then buy the ETF. Never try to time the market.
5. **Cash for payouts.** Keep at least one year of expected payouts and required spending in cash.
6. **Who can act.** Any trade or transfer needs **two directors**. No agent may place trades.
7. **Reporting.** Publish holdings, value, costs, and every transaction at least quarterly on the public ledger.
8. **Review.** Review this policy once a year, and immediately if the legal structure changes.
