# Legal structure options

> **Not legal advice.** This page compares options so the founder and future advisors can have a faster, cheaper conversation with a lawyer. Costs are government fees only, in the currency shown, checked on 2026-09-24. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** were not confirmed from an official source.

## The short answer

- **Right now:** stay an unincorporated open-source project that **accepts no money**. This is free and safe as long as no funds are held.
- **Next:** create a small **non-profit corporation** (Quebec or federal) once there are at least a few committed co-stewards. It is cheap and quick, and it can be self-filed.
- **Then decide** between two paths for real money:
  - **Path A, charity:** apply to become a **registered charity** whose first purpose is relieving poverty with cash. It can issue tax receipts, but payouts must go to people in poverty, not everyone.
  - **Path B, partner:** run the first real fund **inside an existing organization** (a partner charity, or a group like Windfall Trust) and focus E4H on design, outreach, and the open-source software.
- **Later:** add other entities only when there is a clear need, for example a US "friends of" charity for US donors, or a separate company for an investment product.

**The key finding:** in both Canada and the US, charity law does not allow payments to everyone regardless of need. Universal payouts are a long-term goal that will need a different legal basis, such as government policy, an international agreement, or a non-charitable structure that does not offer tax receipts. That is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to start where need is greatest, which is also what Windfall Trust plans to do.

## Option by option

### 1. Stay an unincorporated open-source project (now)

- **What it allows:** publishing ideas, code, and documents; talking to partners; building prototypes with pretend data.
- **What it does not allow:** safely holding anyone else's money. Money in a personal account mixes personal and project funds, has no legal protection for the founder, and gives donors no accountability.
- **Rule for this stage:** do not accept donations, crypto, or "pledges" of any kind. The site already says this, and it should keep saying it.

### 2. Fiscal sponsorship

A fiscal sponsor is an existing organization that "adopts" a project so it can use the sponsor's bank account, legal status, and sometimes tax receipts.

- **In Canada** the rules are strict. A registered charity can only host a project as its *own* program. It must "own" and control the project and its funds. It cannot simply pass money through. ([Metcalf Foundation, Shared Platforms](https://metcalffoundation.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Shared-Platforms-and-CVOs.pdf); [CRA, using an intermediary](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/using-intermediary-carry-a-charitys-activities-within-canada.html))
- **Open Collective** is software for transparent budgets, plus a network of hosts. The US charity host **Open Collective Foundation closed on 31 December 2024** ([announcement](https://opencollective.com/foundation/updates/announcement-we-are-dissolving-open-collective-foundation-at-the-end-of-this-year)). **Open Source Collective** still operates, charges **10% of incoming funds**, only hosts open-source software projects, is a US 501(c)(6) (gifts are not tax-deductible), holds funds only in US dollars, and cannot pay people in sanctioned countries ([OSC projects page](https://oscollective.org/projects/); [is OSC right for me](https://docs.oscollective.org/interested-in-joining-osc/is-osc-right-for-me)).
- **Fit for E4H:** good for funding the *software and operations* (Bucket B in the [administration principles](../current/equity-for-humanity-administration-principles-v0.1.md)). Poor fit for the Humanity Fund itself, because hosts rarely allow investing donations long-term or paying individuals worldwide.
- **Lesson to copy:** Open Collective's public ledger, where every income and expense is visible.

### 3. Quebec non-profit (OBNL, Part III of the Companies Act)

- **Cost:** $199 for letters patent; $27 optional name reservation; $41 annual registration fee in 2026; the annual update declaration is free if filed on time. ([Registraire des entreprises fees](https://www.quebec.ca/entreprises-et-travailleurs-autonomes/tarifs-registraire-entreprises/personne-morale-sans-but-lucratif); [2026 fee schedule RE-101](https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/emploi-solidarite-sociale/registraire_entreprises/RE-101_REQ_Tous_Tarifs_2026.pdf))
- **After filing:** an initial declaration is due within 60 days, free if on time ([Constituer une PMSBL](https://www.quebec.ca/entreprises-et-travailleurs-autonomes/demarrer-entreprise/immatriculer-constituer-entreprise/immatriculer-constituer-entreprise-formes-juridiques/personne-morale-sans-but-lucratif)).
- **People needed:** Part III normally needs at least three applicants **[UNVERIFIED]**. This is a good forcing function: E4H should not be a one-person organization anyway.
- **Pros:** cheapest, local, French-language home, simple.
- **Cons:** cannot issue tax receipts. Operating mainly in French with the Quebec registry.

### 4. Federal non-profit (CNCA)

- **Cost and time:** $200 online, usually 1 day; annual return $12 ([Corporations Canada fees](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/services-fees-and-processing-times); [annual return](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/keep-your-corporation-good-shape/annual-return)).
- **Extra step:** a federal corporation based in Quebec must also register with the Quebec registry and pay its annual fee **[UNVERIFIED exact fee]**.
- **Pros:** recognized across Canada, English or French, well-documented by-law templates.
- **Cons:** two registries to keep current.

### What a non-profit (NPO) can and cannot do with money

Both non-profit options above are usually tax-exempt under the Income Tax Act, paragraph 149(1)(l). Three limits matter for E4H:

- **No personal benefit to members.** No income may be paid to or available for the personal benefit of members or directors ([Income Tax Act s.149](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-149.html)). Paying the public (non-members) is not directly banned, but it is untested for a program like E4H **[UNCERTAIN]**.
- **Investment income is a warning sign.** CRA says incidental investment income is fine, but a surplus "well beyond what is reasonably required" or aggressively pursuing investment income may show a profit purpose and cost the exemption ([CRA interpretation 2024-1045401E5, via Tax Interpretations](https://mail.taxinterpretations.com/cra/severed-letters/2024-1045401e5)). An NPO whose main activity is growing an investment fund is exactly the risky case. **Get advice before an NPO holds a growing fund.**
- **Filing:** the NPO information return (T1044) is required if passive income is over $10,000 or assets over $200,000. Proposed rules starting in 2027 would add a short return for receipts over $10,000 and the full T1044 over $100,000 ([Carters summary](https://www.carters.ca/department-of-finance-releases-new-legislative-proposals-for-npo-reporting/)).

### 5. Canadian registered charity

- **How:** an existing entity (usually a non-profit corporation) applies online through CRA My Business Account. No fee. CRA's standard is an initial response within six months of a complete application; it met that 96% of the time in 2023-24. The average time to a final decision was 147 days in 2022-23. ([CRA service standards](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/service-standards-cra/service-standards-2023-24.html); [CanadianCharityLaw.ca summary](https://www.canadiancharitylaw.ca/blog/charities-directorate-of-cra-releases-report-on-the-charities-program-2022-to-2023/); [how to apply](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/registering-charitable-qualified-donee-status/apply-become-registered-charity/apply/submit-application.html))
- **The big limit, relief of poverty:** CRA guidance CG-029 says a poverty charity must benefit people who are **experiencing poverty now**. Preventing poverty, or helping people "at risk", does not count. Giving more than needed to relieve poverty can be an unacceptable private benefit. Some benefit to non-poor people is allowed only if it is incidental, necessary, reasonable, and proportionate (for example, a school meal program open to all to avoid stigma). ([CG-029](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/charitable-registration-relief-poverty.html); [CRA relief of poverty page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/registering-charitable-qualified-donee-status/apply-become-registered-charity/establishing/charitable-purposes/relief-poverty.html))
- **What that means for E4H:** a charity can pay cash to people in poverty, in Canada or abroad, with records showing how recipients were selected. It **cannot** pay a universal dividend to everyone. A "universal by right, opt-in" design where the non-poor mostly let their share recycle is creative, but it is unlikely to satisfy CRA without selection criteria **[UNCERTAIN; ask a charity lawyer]**.
- **Working outside Canada:** a charity may run its own activities abroad through intermediaries it directs and controls (guidance CG-002), or, since 2022, make "qualifying disbursements" (grants) to non-qualified donees with due diligence and reporting (guidance CG-032). ([CG-002](https://www.canadiancharitylaw.ca/uploads/CRA_Guidance_CG-002,_Canadian_Registered_Charities_Carrying_Out_Activities_Outside_of_Canada_-_2017.pdf); [CG-032](https://www.canadiancharitylaw.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Registered-charities-making-grants-to-non-qualified-donees-CG-032-December-19-2023.pdf))
- **Minimum spending (disbursement quota):** each year a charity must spend at least 3.5% of the average value of its investment assets up to $1 million, and 5% above $1 million. For charitable organizations it applies once those assets average more than $100,000 ([CRA disbursement quota](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/annual-spending-requirement-disbursement-quota/disbursement-quota-calculation.html)). **This clashes with E4H's dynamic payout rule**, which pays 0% in bad years. The rule would need a floor, or spending on charitable activities, or use of past excess spending, to stay compliant.
- **Upkeep:** annual T3010 information return. In Quebec, charities also file with Revenu Québec **[UNVERIFIED form number]**.

### 6. BC benefit company and community contribution company (C3)

- **Benefit company:** a normal for-profit company that commits to public benefits and must publish an annual benefit report against a third-party standard. $350 to incorporate, $30 for a name, $43.39 annual report ([BC benefit company guide](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/employment-business-and-economic-development/business-management/permits-licences-and-registration/registries-packages/information_package_for_benefit_company.pdf); [BC forms and fees](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/permits-licences/businesses-incorporated-companies/forms-corporate-registry)).
- **C3:** a for-profit company with dividends capped at 40% of annual profit; it cannot receive tax-deductible donations and pays tax ([BC incorporated companies](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/permits-licences/businesses-incorporated-companies/incorporated-companies)).
- **Fit for E4H:** only for a future *investment arm* (the "invest for yourself" idea in the concept brief). A Quebec-based company would also need Quebec registration.

### 7. Trust

A trust holds property for a purpose under a trustee. In Quebec a "social trust" (fiducie d'utilité sociale) is possible under the Civil Code; elsewhere, a charitable trust. A charitable trust faces the same poverty and private-benefit limits as any charity. Trusts need careful drafting. **Not researched in depth; lawyer needed.**

### 8. United States options

- **501(c)(3) public charity:** IRS user fee $600 for Form 1023, $275 for the short Form 1023-EZ if eligible ([Form 1023 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023); [1023-EZ instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023ez)). Grants to individuals must serve a "charitable class" chosen by objective, consistent criteria; payments to specific or pre-selected people are private benefit ([1023-EZ instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023ez)). Like Canada, universal payments to everyone would not qualify. Canadian donors generally cannot deduct gifts to US charities except against US-source income, and the reverse is also true ([IRS, Canadian charities under the treaty](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/eotopicc01.pdf)).
- **501(c)(4):** allows advocacy; gifts not deductible.
- **Delaware public benefit corporation (PBC):** $109 to incorporate; $50 annual report plus franchise tax (minimum $175 or $400 depending on method); registered agent typically $50 to $300 per year ([Delaware fee schedule](https://corpfiles.delaware.gov/Fee%5FSchedule/AugustFee2026.pdf); [Delaware franchise tax](https://revenue.delaware.gov/business-tax-forms/franchise-taxes/)).
- **When a US entity makes sense:** only once there is meaningful US donor interest. A common pattern is a US "friends of" 501(c)(3) that makes grants to the Canadian charity. That needs a lawyer.

### 9. Hybrids

The best-known current example is OpenAI: the nonprofit **OpenAI Foundation** controls a for-profit **OpenAI Group PBC** ([OpenAI Foundation update](https://openai.com/index/update-on-the-openai-foundation/)). The lesson for E4H is that a mission-controlled parent can own a business arm. It is also expensive and complex. E4H should not consider a hybrid until there is a real business line and paid professional help.

## Key regulatory issues

| Issue | Plain-language risk | What E4H should do |
|---|---|---|
| **Securities law** | If donors or participants are promised a financial return, or described as "owners" or "shareholders", regulators could see an investment product. Recipients receiving gifts is normally not a security. | Never promise donors any return. Describe payouts as benefits or gifts, not ownership stakes. Have a lawyer review wording before launch. |
| **Money transmission (payments)** | In Canada, anyone in the business of "remitting or transmitting funds" for others must register with FINTRAC as a money services business. Quebec also requires a separate licence from Revenu Québec. Crowdfunding platforms are covered too. ([FINTRAC MSB](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/msb-esm/msb-eng); [Revenu Québec MSB licence](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/sector-specific-measures/money-services-businesses-msbs/money-services-business-licence-application/)) | Pay out only E4H's own funds through a licensed payment provider or an experienced partner such as GiveDirectly. Do not build a wallet or move other people's money. Whether a charity paying its own funds is caught is **[UNCERTAIN]**; confirm with counsel. |
| **Know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering (KYC/AML)** | Donations and payouts can be abused for laundering or to reach sanctioned people. | Use regulated processors for money in and out. Screen large donors. Keep records. No anonymous large gifts. |
| **Investment management registration** | Managing investments *for others* as a business, or running a pooled "investment fund", can require registration as a portfolio manager or investment fund manager. An organization investing its **own** money generally does not trigger this. ([ASC, investment funds](https://www.asc.ca/en/investment-funds/how-do-the-registration-requirements-apply-to-investment-funds); [NI 31-103 companion policy](https://www.asc.ca/-/media/ASC-Documents-part-1/Regulatory-Instruments/2026/01/6258977v1-31-103-CP-Consolidation-Eff-January-1-2026.pdf)) | The E4H entity owns its fund outright. No personal accounts inside the fund. No "your share of the fund" balances that people could treat as their property. See [investing](investing.md). |
| **Tax for recipients** | Whether a payout is taxable income depends on the country and on how it is framed. **[UNCERTAIN]** for Canada and other countries. | Get a written tax opinion before the first payout. Tell recipients plainly. |
| **Privacy and identity** | Verifying unique humans creates sensitive data, especially biometrics. | Prefer privacy-preserving proof of personhood. Collect the minimum. See [open questions](open-questions.md). |
| **Quebec language law** | Public content from a Quebec organization may need to be in French. | Publish French versions of key pages. See [open questions](open-questions.md). |

## Where to spend on a lawyer

Self-filing is fine for: the non-profit incorporation, annual returns, and a business number. A lawyer is genuinely worth it for:

1. **Charitable purposes and by-laws** before applying to CRA (one review, a few hours).
2. **Payout design:** the disbursement quota, the poverty-selection criteria, and the dynamic payout rule.
3. **Payments and securities wording** before any public launch.
4. **Any US entity.**

Low-cost routes to try first: pro bono charity-law programs, law school clinics, and advice through partner organizations **[UNVERIFIED which programs serve Quebec]**. Agents can prepare a short brief so paid time is used efficiently.

## Lessons from comparable efforts

| Example | What it is | Lesson to copy |
|---|---|---|
| **GiveDirectly** | Sends cash to people in poverty, mostly by mobile money. Delivered $126M to over 200,000 recipients in 12 countries in 2024; known fraud losses were 0.19% ([2024 risk report](https://www.givedirectly.org/risk-report-2024)). | Partner for payouts instead of building them. Publish fraud losses openly. |
| **Alaska Permanent Fund** | $91.5B state fund (June 2026). Its draw is based on the average value over five of the past six years, capped at 5% ([APFC](https://apfc.org/the-fund/)). The 2025 dividend was $1,000 ([Alaska PFD](https://pfd.alaska.gov/)). Since 2016 lawmakers have set the dividend themselves instead of following the formula ([Juneau Empire](https://www.juneauempire.com/2025/09/19/alaskas-2025-permanent-fund-dividend-will-be-1000-and-arrives-starting-oct-2/)). | Smooth payouts over several years. Protect the payout rule from being overridden. |
| **Norway's fund (GPFG)** | NOK 22,683 billion (June 2026), about 7,100 companies, and it publishes every holding ([NBIM fund value](https://www.nbim.no/en/investments/the-funds-value/); [all investments](https://www.nbim.no/en/investments/all-investments/)). | Publish every holding. Use an independent ethics process for exclusions. |
| **OpenResearch cash study** | 1,000 people got $1,000 a month for three years. Spending rose about $310 a month, mostly on food, rent, and transport; work fell slightly; mental-health gains faded ([OpenResearch](https://www.openresearchlab.org/projects/unconditional-cash-study); [spending findings](https://www.openresearchlab.org/findings/key-findings-spending)). | Promise modestly. Measure honestly, including disappointing results. |
| **GoodDollar** | A crypto basic income that stakes supporter capital and distributes the yield; face-scan verification; about 800,000 members ([GoodDollar sybil docs](https://docs.gooddollar.org/how-gooddollar-works/sybil-resistance); [GoodDollar 4-year lessons](https://medium.com/gooddollar/learning-through-doing-gooddollars-4th-anniversary-of-crypto-universal-basic-income-82811694ba0d)). A partner project reported coordinated fake accounts in January 2026 ([GoodDollar forum](https://discourse.gooddollar.org/t/cogov-weeks-7-9-infrastructure-mechanisms/8738)). | The closest model to E4H. Verification can be gamed by paying poor people to verify for someone else. Pay in stable money, not a new token. |
| **UK Child Trust Fund** | About 827,000 matured accounts still unclaimed in April 2026, averaging £2,310 each ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/almost-3-million-young-adults-are-quids-in-after-claiming-child-trust-fund)). | People forget money that is not in front of them. Design for reminders and easy claiming. |
| **Donor-advised funds and Every.org** | Donors give to one charity that invests and later grants onward. Every.org charges nonprofits no platform fee but only grants to US 501(c)(3)s or equivalents ([Every.org pricing](https://www.every.org/pricing); [international charities](https://support.every.org/hc/en-us/articles/5527881074579-Do-you-support-international-charities-What-about-fiscally-sponsored-organizations)). | "Give now, invest, grant later" is already legal and familiar to donors. |
| **Open Collective** | Transparent budgets for thousands of projects ([docs](https://documentation.opencollective.com/fiscal-hosts/fiscal-hosts)). | Public ledger from day one. |
