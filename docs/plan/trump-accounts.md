# Trump accounts

> **Working draft.** Facts were checked on 2026-09-24. The rules for these accounts are still being finalized; proposed regulations published in 2026 may change.

## In brief

- **Trump accounts are US government-backed investment accounts for children.** They were created by the 2025 federal tax law (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, section 530A of the Internal Revenue Code) and opened on 4 July 2026.
- **Equity for Humanity is not eligible to contribute.** Only US governments and US 501(c)(3) charities may make general contributions, and only through the US Treasury, in equal amounts to every child in a large defined group.
- **The design offers useful lessons.** These include an early seed deposit, a small number of low-cost index funds, a lock-up until adulthood, and a legal channel for philanthropic contributions at scale. Early participation data also shows the limits of opt-in enrolment.
- **Planned use:** follow developments, apply the design lessons, and refer to the accounts in public education, including discussion of Canadian equivalents.

## Status in September 2026

- The accounts opened on 4 July 2026. By late July, the Treasury reported more than 7 million accounts, of which 1.7 million were eligible for the $1,000 seed deposit, and $1.5 billion invested ([CNN, updated 23 July 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/04/business/trump-accounts-faq)).
- Some families reported delays in receiving the seed deposit. The Treasury stated that most deposits arrive within one or two days ([Washington Times, 22 July 2026](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jul/22/trump-touts-trump-accounts-families-still-waiting-babys-1000/)).
- A September 2026 survey by the nonprofit Commonwealth found that about 5% of eligible low- and moderate-income families had opened an account, although 55% were aware of the program ([Yahoo Finance, 22 September 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/trump-accounts-could-help-lower-income-families-most-few-have-signed-up-143843357.html)).

## The Dell pledge

- In December 2025, Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25 billion to deposit $250 in the accounts of up to 25 million children born between 2016 and 2024, living in ZIP codes with a median family income of $150,000 or less ([CNBC, December 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/02/michael-susan-dell-trump-accounts.html); [Invest America](https://investamerica.org/dell/)).
- Deposits began on 31 August 2026 and are being rolled out nationally. Only the first 25 million activated accounts are eligible ([CNBC, 31 August 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/31/trump-account-holders-to-start-receiving-250-dell-grant-spokesperson.html)).

## Published criticism

- **Opt-in enrolment tends to exclude lower-income families.** The Urban Institute cites Maine's Alfond Grant program, which enrolled about 40% of newborns under opt-in and close to 100% after moving to automatic enrolment in 2013 ([Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/automatic-enrollment-not-opt-only-way-guarantee-trump-accounts-dont-leave-behind)).
- **Family contributions favour households that can already save** ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-trump-accounts-what-are-baby-bonds/); [Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/without-clear-guidelines-trump-accounts-would-mostly-benefit-already-wealthy-families)).
- **"Baby bond" programs,** such as Connecticut's, direct larger public deposits to children with the least family wealth and are therefore more targeted ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-trump-accounts-what-are-baby-bonds/)).

## Possible connections for Equity for Humanity

| Possibility | Assessment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Contributing to Trump accounts | Not feasible | Requires a US 501(c)(3), must be made through the Treasury, and must cover an entire qualified class in equal amounts. |
| Applying design lessons | Feasible | Early seeding, a small number of low-cost index funds, a fee ceiling, a lock-up period, and channels for philanthropic contributions. |
| Exchanging lessons with organizations involved | Possible | Invest America (the Dell partner) and Commonwealth (the take-up researchers) study questions relevant to this project. |
| Public education on Canadian equivalents | Feasible | Canada already has comparable programs, described below. |
| Explaining the difference in design | Feasible | Trump accounts are US-only, invest in US equities only, serve children only, and exclude ESG-screened funds. Equity for Humanity aims to be global, to invest in world markets with a narrow conduct screen, and to include people of all ages. |

## Lessons for the design of Equity for Humanity

1. **Opt-in alone is unlikely to reach the people the project most wants to reach.** The Founding Concept Brief's "connect" step, in which people and local groups help others access the fund, will be essential. Take-up should be measured.
2. **Unclaimed amounts are to be expected.** The Founding Concept Brief provides that unclaimed benefits are reinvested in the fund. Reminders should ensure that leaving a benefit unclaimed is an informed choice.
3. **A small number of low-cost funds is workable at scale.** Trump accounts operate with a 0.1% fee ceiling and five index funds.
4. **Large philanthropic contributions can reach many people when a legal channel exists.** A similar channel could be considered for the Humanity Fund in future.
5. **Screens may be excluded by regulation.** The US proposed rules exclude ESG-focused funds from Trump accounts. Any future connection with government-run accounts may be subject to similar limits.
