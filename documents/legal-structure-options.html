Legal structure

Not legal advice. This page summarizes options so that the founder, volunteers, and future advisors can prepare for legal guidance efficiently. Facts were checked on 2026-09-24. Items marked [UNVERIFIED] were not confirmed from an official source; items marked [UNCERTAIN] depend on facts or interpretations that require professional advice.

In brief

  • At present, Equity for Humanity remains an unincorporated open-source project that accepts no contributions of any kind.
  • Registered charity status does not fit the core fund. The CRA requires charities that relieve poverty to direct their benefits to people in need. The core fund is universal and applies no means test.
  • The core fund will therefore use a non-charity structure. The leading candidate is a not-for-profit corporation, either federal or in the province where the project is based. The specific form will be chosen with legal advice.
  • Other entities will be considered only when there is a clear need. Examples include a region-based charitable sister organization (listed in open questions), a US entity for US supporters, or a separate company for a possible investment arm, as the Founding Concept Brief anticipates.
  • Costs are kept to a minimum. Free or pro bono legal help will be sought first, with a paid consultation only if absolutely necessary.

Why registered charity status does not fit

The Founding Concept Brief states that "every verified human may claim their share" and that "no one must prove need". Canadian charity law takes a different approach.

  • Relief of poverty requires beneficiaries in need. CRA guidance CG-029 states that a charity relieving poverty must provide its benefits to people who are experiencing poverty. Preventing poverty, or assisting people at risk of poverty, is not recognized as a charitable purpose. Benefits beyond what is needed to relieve poverty may be an unacceptable private benefit. (CRA, CG-029 Relief of poverty and charitable registration; CRA, relief of poverty)
  • Selection criteria are expected. A charity may need to apply criteria, such as income or assets, to confirm that recipients are in need, and must keep records showing how recipients were selected (CG-029).
  • The disbursement quota was also considered. The disbursement quota is the CRA's annual minimum spending requirement for registered charities. It is calculated on the average value, over the previous 24 months, of the charity's property not used directly in charitable activities or administration. The rate is 3.5% of that value up to $1 million and 5% on any amount above $1 million. For charitable organizations it applies once that average exceeds $100,000; for foundations, once it exceeds $25,000. (CRA, disbursement quota calculation) The requirement could have been met through charitable activities. The decisive factor was the need-based beneficiary rule, which conflicts with a universal fund.

United States charity law is similar. A 501(c)(3) organization making grants to individuals must serve a "charitable class" selected by objective criteria, such as people in poverty, and may not serve private interests (IRS, Form 1023-EZ instructions).

Consequence: the core fund is planned as a non-charity structure. It will not issue official donation receipts. Contributors should not expect a tax deduction for contributions to the core fund.

Options in detail

Unincorporated open-source project (current)

  • Appropriate for: publishing documents and code, consulting partners, and building prototypes with simulated data.
  • Not appropriate for: holding money on behalf of others. Funds held in a personal account provide no legal protection for the founder and no accountability to contributors.
  • Rule for this stage: no contributions, pledges, or crypto-assets are accepted.

Not-for-profit corporation (proposed for the core fund)

  • Quebec not-for-profit (Part III, Companies Act): letters patent cost $199, with an optional $27 name reservation. The 2026 annual registration fee is $41, and the annual update declaration is free if filed on time (Registraire des entreprises fees; 2026 fee schedule). An initial declaration is due within 60 days of registration (Constituer une personne morale sans but lucratif). The minimum number of applicants is [UNVERIFIED].
  • Federal not-for-profit (Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act): online incorporation costs $200 and is usually processed in one day. The annual return costs $12 (Corporations Canada fees; annual return). A federal corporation operating in Quebec must also register with the Quebec enterprise registry [UNVERIFIED fee].
  • Tax status: not-for-profit organizations are generally exempt from income tax under paragraph 149(1)(l) of the Income Tax Act. They must not make income available for the personal benefit of members (Income Tax Act, section 149).
  • An important uncertainty. The CRA has indicated that incidental investment income is acceptable. However, a surplus well beyond operational needs, or the active pursuit of investment income, may indicate a profit purpose and put the tax exemption at risk (CRA technical interpretation 2024-1045401E5). A fund whose central activity is long-term investment may therefore not qualify for the exemption, and could be taxable. Whether this applies, and how it affects the choice of structure, is a priority question for legal and tax advice [UNCERTAIN].
  • Reporting: the not-for-profit information return (T1044) is currently required when passive income exceeds $10,000 or assets exceed $200,000. Proposed rules for fiscal periods beginning in 2027 would extend reporting to more organizations (Carters, proposed NPO reporting rules).

Fiscal sponsorship

A fiscal sponsor is an established organization that allows a project to operate under its legal and banking arrangements.

  • In Canada, a registered charity may host a project only as its own program. It must control the project and its funds, and may not act as a conduit (Metcalf Foundation, Shared Platforms; CRA, using an intermediary).
  • Open Collective: the US host Open Collective Foundation closed on 31 December 2024 (announcement). Open Source Collective continues to host open-source software projects. It charges 10% of incoming funds, holds funds in US dollars only, and cannot pay recipients in sanctioned countries. As a US 501(c)(6) organization, it cannot offer tax-deductible receipts (Open Source Collective; eligibility).
  • Fit: possibly suitable for the open-source software and operating costs (the Stewardship and Operations Reserve described in the Administration and Stewardship Principles). It is not suitable for the Humanity Fund itself.

Registered charity

  • Process: an existing entity applies online through the CRA's My Business Account; there is no fee. The CRA's service standard is an initial response within six months of a complete application; in 2023–24 it met this standard 96% of the time (CRA service standards; how to apply).
  • Activities abroad: a charity may carry out its own activities outside Canada through intermediaries it directs and controls (guidance CG-002), or make qualifying grants to organizations that are not qualified donees (guidance CG-032) (CG-002; CG-032).
  • Status in this plan: not proposed for the core fund, for the reasons set out above. A separate, region-based charitable organization is listed as an open question.

BC benefit company and community contribution company

  • Benefit company: a for-profit company committed to specified public benefits, which must publish an annual benefit report against a third-party standard. Incorporation costs $350, name approval $30, and the annual report $43.39 (BC benefit company guide; BC forms and fees).
  • Community contribution company: a for-profit company whose dividends are capped at 40% of annual profit. It cannot receive tax-deductible donations and is taxable (BC incorporated companies).
  • Fit: relevant only to the possible future investment arm described in the Founding Concept Brief.

Trust

A trust holds property for a stated purpose under the control of trustees. Quebec's Civil Code provides for a social trust; other provinces use charitable or purpose trusts. A trust could in principle hold a universal fund, but requires careful drafting and has its own tax rules. Not researched in depth; legal advice required.

United States options

  • 501(c)(3) public charity: the IRS user fee is $600 for Form 1023, or $275 for the streamlined Form 1023-EZ where eligible (Form 1023 instructions; Form 1023-EZ instructions). The charitable-class rule described above applies.
  • Delaware public benefit corporation: incorporation costs $109; the annual report costs $50, plus franchise tax with a minimum of $175 or $400 depending on the method; a registered agent is required (Delaware fee schedule; Delaware franchise tax).
  • Cross-border giving: Canadian donors generally cannot deduct gifts to US charities, and US donors generally cannot deduct gifts to Canadian charities, except against income from the other country (IRS, Canadian charities under the treaty).
  • Status in this plan: a US entity would be considered only if there is substantial US support.

Hybrid structures

OpenAI's structure is a current example: the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation controls OpenAI Group PBC (OpenAI Foundation update). A mission-controlled parent can own a business arm, but hybrids are complex and costly. They are not proposed at this stage.

Legal advice

Free or pro bono legal help will be sought first, for example through legal clinics and pro bono programs [UNVERIFIED which programs are available]. A paid consultation will be arranged only if absolutely necessary. Preparation by volunteers and AI agents is intended to keep any advice session short and focused.

The priority questions are:

  1. The most suitable non-charity structure for a universal investment fund, including the tax-exemption uncertainty described above.
  2. Securities and money-services considerations for the payout design and public wording.
  3. Whether a region-based charitable sister organization is worth pursuing, and how it would relate to the core fund.

Routine filings, such as incorporation, annual returns, and a business number, can be completed without a lawyer.

Lessons from comparable efforts

ExampleDescriptionLesson for Equity for Humanity
GiveDirectlyDelivers cash transfers to people in poverty, mainly through mobile money. In 2024 it delivered $126M to more than 200,000 recipients in 12 countries, with known fraud losses of 0.19% (2024 risk report).Use experienced delivery partners, and publish fraud losses openly.
Alaska Permanent FundA state fund of $91.5B at June 2026. Its annual draw is based on the average value over five of the preceding six years, capped at 5% (APFC). The 2025 dividend was $1,000 (Alaska PFD). Since 2016, legislators have set the dividend amount rather than following the statutory formula (Juneau Empire).Smooth distributions over several years, and protect the payout rule from ad hoc changes.
Norway's Government Pension Fund GlobalValued at NOK 22,683 billion at June 2026 and invested in about 7,100 companies. It publishes all of its holdings (NBIM, fund value; NBIM, all investments).Publish all holdings, and use an independent, documented process for exclusions.
OpenResearch Unconditional Cash Study1,000 participants received $1,000 per month for three years. Monthly spending rose by about $310, mainly on food, rent, and transport.
