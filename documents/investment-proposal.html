E4H — Global Human-Flourishing Index Proposal v0.2

Discussion draft — revised 9 July 2026 Status: This is a concept-stage policy proposal, not a fund, securities offering, investment recommendation, or direction to invest real money. It supersedes the v0.1 draft for discussion purposes.

The decision

E4H will use the FTSE Global All Cap Index as its reference universe.

The intended future policy is simple:

  1. Own the broad public productive economy through the FTSE Global All Cap universe: developed and emerging markets; large-, mid-, and small-cap companies; free-float market-cap weights.
  2. Do not try to forecast winners, rank industries morally, or create a solutions tilt. The market sets company weights. Companies that grow become larger positions; companies that decline become smaller positions or leave under FTSE’s published rules.
  3. Apply one narrow conduct floor: omit otherwise eligible companies that Sustainalytics Global Standards Screening (GSS) classifies as non-compliant with the UN Global Compact’s Ten Principles.

This is an ownership framework, not a promise of returns or a claim that every company held is good.

1. Why this is the right starting point

Long-run market gains have been driven by a small number of exceptional companies. No committee, model, or score can reliably identify all of them before they grow.

A global all-cap, market-weighted approach is therefore the simplest honest answer:

  • it includes new eligible public companies through published FTSE rules;
  • it preserves exposure to small, medium, and large businesses across the world;
  • it does not turn E4H into an expensive stock-picking operation; and
  • it keeps the core aligned with broad prosperity, productivity, innovation, and human enterprise.

“Global” means eligible, investable public securities in FTSE-covered markets. It does not mean every country, private company, or illiquid security.

2. Core portfolio rule

Policy elementE4H position
Reference universeFTSE Global All Cap Index.
WeightingFree-float-adjusted market-cap weighting. No discretionary security selection.
Company sizeRetain FTSE’s large-, mid-, and small-cap coverage.
CountriesRetain FTSE’s developed and emerging-market coverage and classifications.
New listingsFollow FTSE’s published eligibility and review rules. Do not hand-pick IPOs.
Positive-impact tiltNone. The core remains market-weighted.
Quality/growth filterNone. E4H will not exclude a company merely because it is early-stage, unprofitable, unfashionable, or judged low-scoring.
Active company steeringNone as a default. E4H will not run a broad activist-engagement programme or attempt to direct company strategy.

The market will make many decisions that E4H would not make itself. That is part of broad ownership. E4H’s role is to keep the system simple, transparent, and within a shared minimum conduct floor.

3. The E4H conduct floor: the UN Global Compact

E4H will use the UN Global Compact’s Ten Principles as its common global conduct framework:

  1. Human rights — Principles 1–2.
  2. Labour — Principles 3–6.
  3. Environment — Principles 7–9.
  4. Anti-corruption — Principle 10.

These principles are not a claim that E4H can label companies morally perfect. They provide the common standard for one narrow, externally operated conduct screen.

The screen and exclusion rule

E4H will use Sustainalytics Global Standards Screening (GSS) status data to identify companies assessed against the UN Global Compact’s Ten Principles and related international standards.

At each quarterly review, an otherwise eligible FTSE Global All Cap constituent that Sustainalytics GSS classifies as non-compliant will be omitted from the E4H screened universe, regardless of company size.

E4H will not exclude a company merely because it is on a watchlist, operates in a particular industry, has a low ESG score, or is unpopular. The rule is limited to the approved non-compliant GSS status.

Return to the universe

A company that is no longer classified as non-compliant by Sustainalytics GSS returns at the next practicable quarterly review, provided it remains otherwise eligible for FTSE Global All Cap.

There is no fixed punishment period and no permanent E4H blacklist. The company’s current GSS status—not the passage of time—determines whether the screen applies.

Data coverage

A company with incomplete GSS coverage is not automatically treated as non-compliant. To preserve broad ownership, it remains in the universe unless and until it receives a non-compliant status. E4H must disclose coverage gaps prominently.

What E4H will not do and future-proofing

E4H will not add a separate list of lifestyle industries, political preferences, product categories, or personal moral judgments. It will not apply the additional energy, vice, weapons, diversity, UFLPA, or other screens bundled into the standard FTSE Global All Cap Choice Index. If the UN Global Compact formally amends its Ten Principles, or if Sustainalytics GSS materially changes the approved standards it applies, E4H will publish a versioned update to this policy and a stated implementation date before applying the change at a future quarterly review.

Light-touch ownership

E4H will not try to manage boards or companies. It will ordinarily let boards, management, owners, and market participants make their decisions. If a future legal structure must vote securities, it may follow a disclosed default policy of voting in accordance with board recommendations, subject to applicable law and fiduciary duties. Any active campaign or exceptional intervention requires a future governance decision.

4. Transparency: the default is openness

Trust requires people to see where E4H capital is invested and how decisions are made.

Subject only to legal, contractual, security, and personal-privacy limits, a future E4H implementation should publish:

  • the benchmark name, methodology version, and implementation date;
  • complete holdings and weights, plus country, sector, and size exposure;
  • every exclusion and reinstatement, the applicable UN Global Compact principle, the relevant GSS status, and effective review date;
  • GSS data-coverage levels and material coverage limitations;
  • the screen methodology, data-source version, and any material correction;
  • portfolio costs, turnover, tracking difference, and implementation limits; and
  • the voting arrangement, if any.

Transparency does not require publishing personal data, confidential evidence, security-sensitive information, or material that would violate law or due process.

5. Use what already exists; do not rebuild it unnecessarily

The FTSE Global All Cap Index already supplies the difficult market machinery: eligibility, free-float adjustment, foreign-ownership limits, corporate actions, country classification, reviews, and new-entry rules.

A simple existing implementation example is Vanguard Total World Stock ETF (VT), which Vanguard states seeks to track the FTSE Global All Cap Index and currently lists a 0.06% expense ratio. It demonstrates that broad, low-cost tracking already exists. It is not a decision today about E4H’s eventual legal vehicle, domicile, custody, tax treatment, or suitability.

Implementation conclusion: a future E4H implementation should license or otherwise obtain authorized access to FTSE Global All Cap data and Sustainalytics GSS status data, then apply only the all-size non-compliant GSS rule described in this proposal. A plain broad-market ETF does not itself apply that screen, and E4H must not assume a public off-the-shelf product already matches this exact rule.

6. What remains for future consideration

These decisions are intentionally parked until E4H’s legal and governance structure exists:

  • who holds formal decision rights and resolves conflicts;
  • the contract, licensing, data-delivery, and correction/escalation process for Sustainalytics GSS status data;
  • whether a regulated partner, existing low-cost tracker plus overlay, institutional mandate, or direct replication is most efficient;
  • custody, tax, licensing, audit, trading, and regulatory requirements; and
  • whether a provider would offer preferential institutional pricing or a fee waiver.

Agents can reduce administration. They do not eliminate trading spreads, custody, tax, data licensing, corporate actions, audit, legal duties, or regulatory responsibility. Directly buying every security is therefore a future operational decision, not an assumed no-fee solution.

7. Safe path before real capital

  1. Concept and research: publish this proposal, invite critique, and make no investment-product claims.
  2. Paper implementation: test the all-size Sustainalytics GSS non-compliant screen against the FTSE Global All Cap reference universe; report coverage, turnover, cost, data changes, and limitations.
  3. Structure and advice: obtain qualified legal, fiduciary, tax, licensing, data, custody, and governance advice.
  4. Independent decision: only a properly constituted future body may decide whether a lawful implementation is warranted.

Bottom line

E4H will own the world’s public productive economy broadly through FTSE Global All Cap, let the market set weights, omit only companies with a Sustainalytics GSS non-compliant UN Global Compact status, and show people exactly what it owns and why.

That is simple enough to administer, broad enough to capture future growth, and restrained enough not to substitute one founder’s preferences for humanity’s judgment.
