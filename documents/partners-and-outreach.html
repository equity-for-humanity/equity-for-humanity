Partners and outreach

Working draft. No correspondence has been sent. All messages below are drafts for founder approval. Contact routes marked "verified" were confirmed on the organization's website on 2026-09-24; others still require confirmation.

In brief

  • The first aim is learning, not funding. Equity for Humanity has no legal entity, so most funders cannot yet support it. Initial requests are for feedback, advice, and introductions.
  • Windfall Trust is a similar initiative to learn from and possibly work with. It is developing a global dividend fund with a comparable purpose.
  • Requests are brief and specific, with one question per message and a link to the published plan.

Organizations named in the brief for this plan

OpenAI Foundation

  • About: the nonprofit that controls OpenAI Group PBC. It expects to invest at least $1 billion over the coming year across life sciences, jobs and economic impact, AI resilience, and community programs, as part of a $25 billion commitment (OpenAI Foundation update; Resilience in the age of AI).
  • Current programs: the AI Resilience program focuses on biosecurity, cybersecurity, model safety, and the effects of AI on young people. A Jobs and Economic Futures program was announced in 2026. The 2026 People-First AI Fund ($50 million) was open only to eligible US 501(c)(3) community organizations and closed on 15 July 2026 (People-First AI Fund 2026).
  • Relevance: Equity for Humanity addresses how the benefits of AI-driven growth could be broadly shared, which corresponds most closely to the Jobs and Economic Futures program.
  • Proposed request: none at present. Once a legal entity exists, enquire whether that program supports research on shared ownership. In the meantime, follow its announcements.
  • Contact route: no open call applicable to this project was found. Announcements are published at openaifoundation.org (verified).

World (World Foundation and Tools for Humanity)

  • About: World ID is a proof-of-personhood system. A person verifies once at an Orb, an iris-imaging device, and can then prove online that they are a unique person without disclosing their identity. World reports nearly 18 million Orb-verified people in 160 countries, and more than 39 million World IDs, about half of them verified (World ID announcement; World ID App). World App, which hosts third-party "mini apps", is available in more than 150 countries (World FAQ).
  • Relevance: the Founding Concept Brief lists World ID among the verification methods to explore. It is designed to allow one claim per person.
  • Considerations: Orb access is limited in many regions. Data-protection authorities in several countries have suspended or restricted World's operations [UNVERIFIED list of countries]. Reliance on a single private provider would create a dependency, so more than one verification method should be supported.
  • Requirements for a mini app, according to World's developer documentation (docs.world.org):
    1. Register the application in the World Developer Portal.
    2. Build a mobile-first web application with the MiniKit software development kit.
    3. Use the "verify" command with an action that permits one proof per person.
    4. Pass World's review. Applications must be a final version, "not a demo, trial or beta", must not use the word "World" in their name, and are responsible for complying with the law in every country where they are shown.
    5. In-app payments use crypto tokens on World's network.
  • Implication: the current prototype uses simulated data and cannot be submitted for listing. A private test version could be built to evaluate the verification flow.
  • Proposed request: at a later stage, a discussion of World ID as one of several verification options for a pilot.
  • Contact route: the World Developer Portal at developer.worldcoin.org (verified in documentation).

Moonshots podcast

  • About: a technology podcast hosted by Peter Diamandis with Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin, and Alexander Wissner-Gross. It reports more than one million listeners and more than 250 episodes (moonshots.com). A March 2026 episode with Andrew Yang discussed basic income and AI-related job loss (episode #236).
  • Relevance: the audience follows discussions of AI-driven abundance. Equity for Humanity addresses how the benefits of that growth could be shared.
  • Proposed request: not a guest appearance at this stage. Once the first videos are published, a short note proposing the topic of universal ownership for a future discussion.
  • Contact route: press@moonshots.com (verified). No public guest application form was found.

Approach to outreach

  • Batches: agents prepare several drafts at a time for the founder to review and send together.
  • Brevity: one request per message, under about 150 words, with a link to the plan.
  • Records: each contact, request, and reply is logged in the status tracker.
  • Follow-up: one follow-up after about two weeks, then no further contact unless invited.
  • Accuracy: no message may imply that Equity for Humanity is a charity, accepts contributions, or offers any return.

Draft message 1: similar initiative (for example, Windfall Trust)

Subject: Equity for Humanity: request for an introductory conversation

Dear [Name],

I lead Equity for Humanity, a volunteer-led, open-source initiative proposing a permanent public-benefit fund. Contributions would be invested in a broad global index, and a portion of the returns would be distributed to verified individuals, with unclaimed amounts reinvested. The initiative is at the concept stage: it has no legal entity and holds no funds.

Your work on a global dividend fund addresses a similar question. We would value the opportunity to learn from your approach and to explore whether our work could be useful to yours. Would you be available for a 20-minute conversation in the coming weeks?

Our published plan is available at equityforhumanity.org/documents/.

Yours sincerely, [Founder name] Equity for Humanity
