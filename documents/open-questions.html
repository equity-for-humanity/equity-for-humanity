Open questions

Working draft. This page records decisions taken, questions still open, and known gaps. AI agents add new questions as they arise; the founder, and later the board, resolves them. The founding documents take precedence over any entry on this page.

In brief

  • Decided: no contributions until the legal structure and safeguards exist; the core fund will use a non-charity structure; distributions will be made only through regulated partners.
  • Most important open questions: the specific non-charity structure and its tax status; whether a region-based charitable sister organization is worthwhile; how to verify unique individuals fairly and privately; and how to work with similar initiatives such as Windfall Trust.

Open questions

1. Tax status of the core fund

The CRA has indicated that a not-for-profit organization that accumulates surplus well beyond its operational needs, or actively pursues investment income, may be considered to have a profit purpose and lose its tax exemption. A fund whose central activity is long-term investment may therefore be taxable. The consequences, and the most suitable structure, require legal and tax advice.

2. A region-based charitable sister organization

A separate registered charity could, in principle, support people in need within a defined region, for example through cash transfers delivered with experienced partners, while the core fund remains universal. Questions to consider:

  • whether such an organization would add enough value to justify its governance and reporting obligations;
  • how it would relate to the core fund, so that the Humanity Fund remains separate and is never used for administration;
  • how it would meet CRA requirements, including need-based selection of beneficiaries and the disbursement quota, the CRA's annual minimum spending requirement for registered charities;
  • whether an existing charity could fulfil the same role through a partnership.

No decision has been taken.

3. Tax treatment for recipients

Tax treatment follows each recipient's local rules and still needs confirmation. Before any distribution, the project will obtain advice and inform recipients that they are responsible for their own tax obligations.

4. Public wording and securities law

If participants were told that they "own" part of the fund, a regulator could view the arrangement as an investment. The project's name can remain, but descriptions of distributions should refer to benefits and a portion of returns, and should be reviewed by a lawyer before launch.

5. French-language requirements

Quebec's Charter of the French Language, as amended in 2022, requires commercial documents made available to the public by enterprises established in Quebec, including websites and social media content, to be available in French on terms at least as favourable as any other language (OQLF presentation of the amended law; OQLF guide to social media). Whether this applies to a non-commercial, pre-launch project is [UNCERTAIN]. French versions of key pages are planned regardless.

6. Relationship with similar initiatives

Windfall Trust is developing a global dividend fund with a comparable aim. The project intends to learn from its work and to explore whether the two initiatives could work together. The form of any collaboration is open.

Known gaps

Verification of unique individuals

  • Issue: without verification, one person could claim many times. Strict verification can exclude people without identity documents or smartphones and creates sensitive data.
  • Evidence: GoodDollar uses face verification, yet a partner project reported coordinated fraudulent accounts in January 2026, apparently created by paying people to verify on someone else's behalf (GoodDollar forum). World ID offers strong uniqueness guarantees with privacy protections, but Orb access is limited and some regulators have restricted its operations (see partners). GiveDirectly relies on local enrolment and mobile-money checks (2024 risk report).
  • Direction: the Founding Concept Brief lists World ID, government identification with liveness checks, and supporting signals such as phone and device checks. More than one method should be supported, and no single provider should control access.

Fairness of distribution rules

  • Questions include equal amounts per person, the treatment of children (guardian-managed under the Founding Concept Brief), and what happens when claims exceed available returns.
  • Direction: publish options and invite public comment before any rule is adopted by the board.

Governance

  • At present one person takes decisions. That is acceptable for a concept, but not for a fund.
  • Direction: the governance safeguards listed in the Administration and Stewardship Principles, including independent oversight, conflict-of-interest and compensation policies, and the founder's recusal from decisions on their own compensation.

Transparency reporting

  • Direction: a public ledger from the first transaction; quarterly publication of holdings and exclusions, as the Investment Proposal requires; and an annual report on costs by category, distributions, fraud losses, and take-up. Norway's Government Pension Fund Global, which publishes all of its holdings, is a reference model (NBIM, all investments).

Market downturns

  • Equity markets can fall substantially.
  • Direction: the dynamic payout rule uses a five-year trailing average and reduces distributions to zero when average growth is zero or negative. Alaska's Permanent Fund uses a comparable multi-year average (APFC). Public materials should state clearly that distributions may fall to zero.

Donation receipts

  • Because the core fund is planned as a non-charity structure, contributions to it will not qualify for official Canadian donation receipts. Public materials must not suggest otherwise.

Supporters in other countries

  • Tax treatment of contributions depends on each contributor's country. Cross-border giving services exist but add cost.
  • Direction: consider additional routes only when there is clear demand.

Accessibility

  • The website and application should follow WCAG 2.2 AA where practicable, including captions, contrast, keyboard navigation, plain language, and French. People without smartphones will need assisted access through the "connect" step described in the Founding Concept Brief.

Credibility

  • Contributors commonly look for a legal entity, an independent board, financial statements, clear rules, and limits on individual control. The plan addresses each of these before any contribution is accepted.

Impact measurement

  • Possible measures include the share of each dollar reaching participants, take-up among people with the lowest incomes, participant well-being, fund growth relative to cumulative contributions, and fraud losses.
  • Direction: agree measures before any pilot begins, and publish results in full, as OpenResearch did (OpenResearch).
